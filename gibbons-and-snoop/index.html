Week 4 · Educational videos

Gibbons, narrated by Snoop Dogg

A short introduction to gibbon biology, movement, social behavior, and conservation, told with an unexpected narrator.

How I made it

For this assignment, I chose the AI-assisted route. I used Navigator Prompt to plan the learning goals and topics, then brought that prompt to ChatGPT to draft the script. I asked it to cover brachiation, diet, and conservation, and later to rewrite the narration in Snoop Dogg’s voice because I thought the idea would be funny.

I generated the voiceover with LazyBird and made edits in its editor. Then I brought the audio and wildlife clips I found on Reddit and Tenor into CapCut and lined up the visuals with the narration. It took some careful timing, but I was decently satisfied with the result.

Project brief

The learning goals and direction

The assignment called for a two-to-three-minute instructional video for general learners with little or no background in primatology. The prompt below records the content and production direction I gave the AI tools.

View the AI video prompt and requirements

Prompt via Navigator Prompt: Create a two-to-three-minute instructional video titled “Gibbons: The Acrobatic Apes of the Rainforest.” Introduce learners to the biology, behavior, and ecological significance of gibbons (family Hylobatidae).

Learning objectives

By the end of the video, learners should be able to:

  1. Identify gibbons as apes rather than monkeys.
  2. Explain how gibbons use brachiation to move through rainforest canopies.
  3. Describe basic aspects of gibbon diet, vocal communication, and social behavior.
  4. Identify major threats to gibbon populations and explain why conservation matters.

Planned sequence

  1. Introduce gibbons and distinguish them from monkeys.
  2. Explain how their anatomy supports brachiation.
  3. Describe their rainforest habitat and primarily fruit-based diet.
  4. Introduce vocalizations, territorial songs, and coordinated duets.
  5. Describe common social structures while noting that behavior varies by species.
  6. Explain threats such as habitat loss and illegal wildlife trade.
  7. End with a summary and a call to protect gibbon habitat.

Production and accuracy requirements

  • Pair concise narration with relevant wildlife footage or visual demonstrations, and introduce scientific terms in context.
  • Use on-screen labels, arrows for brachiation, and sound-wave visuals for communication; include rainforest sounds and music where appropriate.
  • Use an engaging but professional narration style, avoid unsupported claims and unnecessary visual distractions, and distinguish general traits from species-specific behavior.
  • Base content on peer-reviewed research, include brief in-scene citations, and provide an APA 7 reference list at the end.
  • Deliver a narrated script, scene-by-scene visual sequence, on-screen text, a closing summary, and references in a visually engaging, accessible video.

Narration drafts

The two script versions

I first drafted a straightforward educational narration, then adapted it into the playful voice used in the finished video.

Read the initial educational narration

Introduction · 0:00–0:15

Have you ever seen an animal swing through the rainforest like an acrobat? Meet the gibbon! Gibbons are small apes that live in the forests of Southeast Asia. Although they may look like monkeys, gibbons are actually apes, just like orangutans, gorillas, and chimpanzees.

Movement and anatomy · 0:15–0:45

One of the most remarkable features of gibbons is their ability to swing through the trees. This movement is called brachiation. Gibbons have exceptionally long arms and specialized muscles that help them swing efficiently from branch to branch. Their powerful forelimbs allow them to travel rapidly through the forest canopy while searching for food.

Diet and habitat · 0:45–1:10

Gibbons spend most of their lives high in the trees. Their diet consists primarily of fruit, but they also eat leaves, flowers, and occasionally insects. By traveling through the canopy and consuming fruit, gibbons can help disperse seeds throughout the forest, contributing to the ecosystem.

Communication · 1:10–1:40

Gibbons are also famous for their songs. Many species produce loud, complex vocalizations, and some perform coordinated duets with their partners. These songs are more than just sounds. They can help gibbons defend their territory, communicate with other groups, and even signal the presence of predators.

Social behavior · 1:40–2:05

Many gibbons live in small family groups consisting of a breeding pair and their offspring. They often form long-term pair bonds and use vocalizations to maintain contact and defend their territory. Their social behavior makes them particularly interesting to researchers studying primate communication and relationships.

Conservation and conclusion · 2:05–2:30

Unfortunately, gibbons face serious threats, including habitat destruction and illegal wildlife trade. Protecting rainforest habitats is essential for their survival. Gibbons are fascinating examples of how animals adapt to their environments. Their incredible movement, complex communication, and social behavior remind us why rainforest conservation matters.

End screen

Remember: Protect the forest, protect the gibbons!

Read the Snoop Dogg narration used in the video

“Yo, what’s good, everybody? Today we’re slidin’ through the rainforest to check out one of the smoothest creatures in the jungle: the gibbon. These little homies ain’t monkeys—they’re apes. And when it comes to movin’ through the trees? Man, they got that whole situation on lock.”

“Check out them arms, though. Long, powerful, built for the ultimate jungle swing. Gibbons use a technique called brachiation—that’s a fancy scientific word for swingin’ from branch to branch like they own the place. They can travel crazy fast through the canopy, barely even touchin’ the ground. While you out here tryna climb a ladder, these dudes are flyin’ through the forest like it’s nothin’. Straight-up acrobatic legends.”

“Now, where these homies hang out? Tropical forests across Southeast Asia. And what’s on the menu? Mostly fruit, with some leaves, flowers, and insects thrown in for flavor. These gibbons keep it fresh, keep it natural, and keep it movin’.”

“But they ain’t just out here snackin’. When they eat fruit, they spread seeds throughout the forest. That means these little tree-swingin’ legends help grow the next generation of rainforest. Nature’s gardeners, baby.”

“And don’t get it twisted—these dudes got pipes. Gibbons sing loud, complex calls that echo through the jungle. Some pairs even perform duets together. That’s right. Couple goals in the canopy. They use these songs to communicate, defend their territory, and let everybody know, ‘Yo, this is our spot. Respect the boundaries.’”

“Gibbons usually roll in small family groups, often with a pair of adults and their young. They spend time together, look out for each other, and keep the family vibes strong. Ain’t no massive squad needed. Just a tight-knit crew, some good branches, and a whole lotta rainforest.”

“But here’s where things get serious, fam. Deforestation and habitat loss are putting gibbons in danger. Their forest homes are disappearin’, and some species are critically endangered. We gotta protect these forests if we want these smooth-swingin’ homies to keep doin’ their thing.”

“So next time you look up at the trees, remember the gibbon: the king of the canopy, the master of the swing, and a true rainforest OG. Protect the forest, protect the gibbons. Stay cool, y’all.”
